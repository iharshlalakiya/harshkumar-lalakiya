From that point on, every GPS fix shows the logger running exactly 6 hours behind true UTC — consistent with the clock falling back to local Mountain Daylight Time instead of UTC and never being corrected. The weather readings themselves look fine; only the clock is wrong. Shift timestamps from Mar 24 16:00 onward forward by 6 hours before using this station in any time-aligned comparison.

2. Where should the technician go next week?

Send them to S03 — Valley Library
  1. Check first: the temperature sensor's cable and connector at the logger. A connection that is intermittently open produces exactly what the data shows: brief readings that snap to a fixed fault value (−40 or 85 °C), recurring roughly weekly, plus one stretch where it stuck at a single value for over three days instead of faulting cleanly.
  2. If the connector and cable look fine, swap the temperature sensor itself — the fault pattern only affects temperature, not the other channels on the same logger, which points at the sensor or its lead rather than the logger board.
  3. While there, do a quick visual check of the radiation shield (spider webs were noted here on a routine visit last month) since it is a five-minute job on the same trip.
Why not S02 (the next most obvious candidate)? S02's pressure problem is a known, fully-diagnosed calibration issue — the board was already swapped, it just needs a reference instrument and ten minutes to calibrate. It doesn't cost any data in the meantime beyond pressure. S03's fault is still active, costs an entire channel for stretches of days at a time, keeps recurring, and the cause has to be found on site. It is the station actually losing data every week, so it gets the one hour this week.
